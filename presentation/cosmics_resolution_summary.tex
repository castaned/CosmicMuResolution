\section{Selection Strategy}

\begin{frame}{Goal of This Update}
  \threebulletsummary
    {Compare a loose/relaxed selection against a tight symmetric tag--probe selection.}
    {Use control plots to understand whether tag/probe differences are driven by asymmetric selection or by kinematics such as probe $p_{T}$.}
    {Summarize the final MC vs Data comparison for the key observables: $p_{T}$ mean and $p_{T}$ sigma, for both DGL and DSA.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Scenario Definitions}
  \begin{block}{Symmetric workflow}
    \tightLooseTable
  \end{block}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The tag/probe identity still comes from the ntuplizer.
    \item The tight scenario adds the same quality cuts to both legs of the pair.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Legacy Reference}
  \begin{block}{Original three-scenario study}
    \legacyTable
  \end{block}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Useful as a historical baseline.
    \item The symmetric workflow is intended to reduce tag/probe quality bias.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Takeaways}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The tight symmetric scenario is designed to reduce track-quality asymmetry between tag and probe.
    \item The new control plots test whether the remaining differences are due to quality cuts or to different kinematics.
    \item The symmetric residual diagnostic provides a direct cross-check on whether the original residual definition is amplifying asymmetry.
    \item The final interpretation should be based on both the control plots and the MC vs Data behavior for DGL and DSA.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
